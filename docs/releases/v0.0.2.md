# PSGopher v0.0.2

The second release of PSGopher, Gopher64 as a native PlayStation 5 title (`PPSA99280`): 64DD disks,
romhacks, game folders, covers by game ID and more overclock and picture options. Still an early
build: expect bugs.

## Added
- **64DD disks**: Gopher64 gains a 64DD, ported from ares: the drive, its clock, `.ndd`, MAME raw
  and `.d64` images (also when named `.n64`), save states with the disk, and disk writes kept beside
  your saves (the image is never changed). Disk images inside a `.zip` or `.7z` start too. It needs
  your own 4 MB dump of the 64DD's IPL: choose it in BENCH > Emulation > 64DD IPL, or put it in
  `/data/psgopher/data/64dd`. IPL files are not listed as games.
- **Romhacks**: `.bps`, `.ips` and `.ups` patches are listed as cartridges of their own and applied
  when they start. Put the patch beside its ROM with the same name, or anywhere for a BPS or UPS
  patch (its ROM is found by size and checksum, also inside a No-Intro-named `.zip` or `.7z`). Or
  choose any patch on a game's page (Triangle > Start With a Patch). Your ROM is never changed.
- **ROMs past 64 MB**, up to the cartridge space's 252 MB, held in memory once while they run.
- **Game Folders** (BENCH > Console): search only the folders you choose, on the console or a USB
  drive, instead of all of `/data` and every drive.
- **Covers and descriptions by the cartridge's game ID** and revision (No-Intro's names), whatever
  the ROM file is called; a variant with no cover of its own (a kiosk demo, a beta, a re-release)
  shows the retail game's.
- **A region filter** in RACK (R3): USA, Europe (PAL), Japan, other.
- **Overclock Speed** (BENCH > Emulation, and per game): 125 MHz (as before) up to 468.75 MHz.
- **Picture**: Final Filter's Bicubic and Lanczos, Post Anti-Aliasing (FXAA) and Sharpening (AMD
  FidelityFX CAS), all off by default.
- **Timing settings for romhacks** (BENCH > Emulation, and per game), all at Accurate by default:
  **VI Timing** Compatible gives a video mode no television could show the 60 pictures a second most
  emulators give it (a large romhack set one, and ran at about a tenth of its speed while the log said
  100%); **CPU Timing** Fast leaves out the CPU's memory waits; **RDP Timing** Instant makes drawing
  take no emulated time. The boot log's `video: emulated time:` line shows where the emulated N64's
  time goes.

## Changed
- BENCH's PC Only section is gone, and so are Emulate UNFLoader and Use DirectInput, which did
  nothing on the console.
- A large USB drive is searched N64-named folders first; other titles' folders in `/data/homebrew`
  are not searched.
- Gopher64 is at `240ccf441ddb`; libnfs at `7306d5f9b8d3`.

## Fixed
- A ROM whose header has a homebrew save type the emulator does not know, or a game ID that is not
  text (seen in romhacks), starts instead of ending with an error.
- A change to Game Folders or the shares during a search is no longer lost, and an unplugged drive's
  games are no longer listed.

## Tested
On a PS5: development builds of this code. A 96 MB romhack (a BPS on a network share, its base ROM in a
`.7z`) was listed, applied and played; with CPU Timing Fast it reached 15-30 fps, and the cause of the
rest was found in the lockstep harness on the PC: its video mode. VI Timing Compatible brought it to 30
fps with full sound in the harness at the N64's 93.75 MHz; that is not yet run on a console. A 64DD disk (in a `.7z` on a network share) booted with
the Japanese IPL chosen in BENCH and ran at 100% and 30 fps at 8x. A 96 MB romhack applied from a network share and ran,
the emulator at full speed at 4x and 8x with the clock at 150 and 281.25 MHz; `NSME` and `NN6E` at
100% at 8x, `NSME` with FXAA and Sharpening on; a 64DD disk named `.n64` was recognised and started
as a disk; covers by game ID; 468.75 MHz (ordinary games are better at 125-150 MHz); Bicubic,
Lanczos (about 3.3 ms of GPU a frame at 8x without Downsampling), FXAA and Sharpening. Not yet run
on a console: the region filter and libnfs's new revision. On a PC: the host tests, the interface's pictures, the check of
the settings' words, the 64DD's tests with synthetic disks, the picture filters against CPU
references, and the lockstep harness (cartridge games run as in Gopher64's own interpreters).

## Installation
- Copy the `PPSA99280` folder to `/data/homebrew/` on the console, over the older one. (Check for
  Updates in v0.0.1 installs the program but not the new `gamedata/nointro-ids.tsv`, so covers stay
  looked up by the file's name until the folder is copied; from v0.0.2 on, updates bring `gamedata/`
  too.)
- Your games, saves, probes and settings are kept (they are in `/data/psgopher`).
- Read `README.md` and `NOTICE.txt` in the folder.

## Reporting problems
- Open an issue and attach `psgopher-boot.log` from the title folder. Don't attach games, BIOS or
  IPL files, firmware or keys.
