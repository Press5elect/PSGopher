Nothing here is decoration

The board follows the real console's layout, and every trace is one of its real buses: the cartridge talks to the RCP, the CPU only through the RCP, the controllers through the PIF. When the title runs, the pulses and chip meters would be driven by the counters the port already records: CPU thread time, GPU waits, audio queue. In this prototype they are simulated.

Settings are parts

Open Bench. Overclocking swaps the crystal, the memory option fills the expansion bay, upscaling is a stage on the video line. Each one is a real Gopher64 option shown where it lives.

The rack holds everything

Open Rack: the whole collection on one screen, one spine per cartridge. Shrink-wrapped spines have never been played, lit ones were played this week, ticks are save states. Sorting moves the cartridges; filtering only dims them, so a game never loses its place.

The gopher

PSGopher keeps Gopher64's logo, credited to the Gopher64 project (GPL-3.0). It is the console icon, sits beside the name, and is printed on the board where the real maker put its mark.

No box art needed

Each cartridge label is generated from the game's own file, so every game looks different without downloading anything. The readout shows what the emulator detects: chip type, save memory, size. Names and figures here are invented examples.
